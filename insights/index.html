Reading the Archive

What the lineages tell us

I read the whole archive as a map: themes, lineages, artists, visitors, motifs, and the memories that link one work to another. Every work carries its own metadata, and from it an ontology can be built and asked questions. Here is what that map says about how the work changes, and, since every session is on record, how long an idea has to wait before the room lets it change anything.

A

Ari's note. Three things stand out. Re-evolution changes a work the most, because it returns to an old parent on purpose. Visitors tend to ask for things to be kept or to arrive, while the artists, left alone, remove and disrupt. And each artist keeps a recognisable hand across both themes, which you can check against the tags yourself. And a lost proposal is rarely the end of it: more than a third are realised later, by a vote or by an artist who went back to the record.
