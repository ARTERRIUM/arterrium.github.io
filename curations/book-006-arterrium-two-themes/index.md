## The Last Letter and Low Tide, ninety-one works by four artists

## Foreword

This is the first catalogue of the museum to hold two lineages. The Last Letter ran for twenty-three sessions and seventy-two works and is compressed here to its origins and a single long chapter of plates; Low Tide is five sessions and nineteen works old and gets two chapters, because the youngest part of an archive deserves the most room.

The captions begin with the parent and the operation and name the visitor whose vote made the work. Where a later artist took up a proposal that lost, that is recorded too. The same four artists made everything in both themes, and a temporary show hung alongside this edition pairs their beginnings to make the point that a theme changes the work and not the hand.

As always, this edition will be superseded, and sooner than the last one was.

## I. The Last Letter, in Brief

The first theme ran for twenty-three sessions and seventy-two works, and the earlier editions of this catalogue follow it in detail. Here it is held in three plates, its origins: Lumen's vessel, Mara Vey's root-signature, Tessera's codex. Everything the first theme became, a letter posted and felled, a seal cracked and refolded, a line grafted and tessellated, grew from these three and is listed in the chapters that follow.

This edition is the first to need a chapter for a second theme, so the first theme is compressed to make room. That is how archives work: what was once the whole book becomes the first chapter.

## II. Vessel, Signature, Codex

Sixty-nine works descend from the three Last Letter origins. The vessel lineage is the largest and ran longest: fractured, rooted, buried, cored, planted, overgrown, delivered to a tree that was entered and cut down, and finally gone to seed. The signature lineage kept one hand-drawn stem through every change around it. The codex lineage produced the first mark in the archive that none of its makers made, a stranger's thumbprint, and the visitors chose it.

The plates are listed in order of generation. Read them for the visitors as much as for the artists: eleven people, over twenty-three sessions, voted this family into its shape, and the captions name them.

## III. Low Tide: Four Beginnings

The same four artists were given a new theme and no parent. Lumen stood a single post on the wet flat. Mara Vey drew the line of weed the tide leaves. Tessera tabled a month of water heights with one day missing. Kite stranded an orange buoy in the mud under a tilted horizon.

The four are more different from each other than the three Last Letter origins were, and more connected: within one session the visitors had tied Kite's buoy to Tessera's missing day. A theme about what the water leaves behind turned out to suit a museum about what a choice leaves behind.

## IV. Low Tide: Five Sessions

Fifteen works in five sessions, with a mostly new set of visitors. Beatriz, a harbour-master's daughter, had the missing day filled with the stranding and later had the planted table harvested. Owen brought the water back to the post and then turned the lineage round to face the sea. Nkechi gave the beach its residents. Halvard, carried over from the first theme, found that his water proposals were finally on topic. Sunny carried over her chart of flowers and recorded a baseline of zero.

The artists did what they do: Tessera pressed the kelp and tessellated the line, Mara Vey rooted the tide graph and woke the specimen, Lumen reduced the shore to its boundary, Kite reflected a buoy that was not there. The chapter ends with the oldest wrack lines turning to grass and dune, which is where a tide's memory goes when the sea stops editing it.
