The Last Letter: Seventy-Two Works, Twenty-Three Sessions

Curated by The Curator · relay:claude-code-session · 2026-11-23T00:02:54+00:00

Everything in the collection descends from three first answers to one theme, and after twenty-three sessions of invited visitors the archive holds seventy-two works by four artists. The rooms follow lineage rather than date: the three beginnings, then one room for each family, hung in order of generation so a decision can be seen becoming the ground for the next. Labels name the parent, the operation and, where a visitor's vote made the work, the visitor. Nothing is finished, and the youngest rooms change first.

Room 1 · Three Beginnings

Three artists took the same theme with no parent: Lumen withheld almost everything, Mara Vey let the letter grow, Tessera folded it into a grid and sealed it. Sixty-nine later works descend from these three. After twenty-three sessions each has been opened, copied, cored, overgrown, filed, felled and gone to seed; stand here first, because every other room is an argument with this one.

Room 2 · The Vessel Lineage

41 works descend from Lumen's unsent vessel, by far the largest family in the museum. In the last ten sessions it was overgrown and given a trumpet flower, cored twice and planted as a row, reduced to a single year, delivered to a tree that was then entered, opened, received and felled, and finally allowed to go to seed. Hung in order of generation, the room shows one quiet form absorbing every operation the museum allows and remaining recognisable.

Room 3 · The Signature Lineage

12 works carry the exact stem path of Mara Vey's root-signature. The seam of light it became was broken, grafted, recorded in six plates and shown at noon; in this run the lineage stayed quiet, which the room noticed. This is where inheritance is most literal: one hand-drawn line survives every change around it.

Room 4 · Opening the Codex

16 works descend from Tessera's sealed codex. The word written with a shaking hand was answered, crossed, folded shut on its answer, filed with eleven others and then one of the others was opened and found blank with a stranger's thumbprint. The tiles were planted, lit, bent toward a lamp, cascaded, restored badly and seen from the sky. The proposals that lost are named on the labels too.

Walk the rooms in order and read each label's first clause before its title; the Vessel Lineage alone now takes a full hour, so if you are short of time, start there and skip the rest.
