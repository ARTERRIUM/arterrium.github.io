## One hundred and fifty-three works, three themes, and the first poems in the archive

## Foreword

This is the ninth catalogue and the first to hold poems. The chapters that came before are kept as they were: the seven beginnings of the first two themes, The Last Letter and Low Tide in order of lineage, and the Crossings made when the two rooms met. The new chapter belongs to a theme that was given a brief instead of a word, and to works that could take either of two forms.

What changed is small to state and large to hang: a parent may now be a poem, and a child may answer it as a picture, or the other way round. The captions say which, and name the Arterrian whose proposal carried and how many times it was asked. The record of what did not carry is kept with the sessions, where the next evolution will find it.

## I. Seven Beginnings

Two themes, seven first answers, all by the same four artists. The three Last Letter origins were a vessel of withheld light, a root-signature and a sealed codex; the four Low Tide origins a post on the flat, a wrack line, a tide table with a missing day and a stranded buoy. Everything else in this book descends from one of them, and the last chapter descends from several at once.

## III. Low Tide

The second theme, fifteen sessions and forty-five descendants: the post through a whole tide, the wrack line multiplied and pressed and sectioned, the table rooted and harvested and dealt, and a person walking out to a buoy and reaching it. ## IV. Crossings

Five sessions with the whole archive on the table and both rooms in one. Fourteen Arterrians proposed, and every proposal that carried crossed between the themes: Rosa's seeds landing on the flat, Halvard's walker posting the buoy in the vessel's absence, Ji-woo's seam giving way, Owen's one sentence between two walkers, Beatriz's reading of the flooded codex on her third attempt. Three of the five winners had lost before; the record kept them until the room was ready.

The artists crossed on their own as well. Mara Vey flooded the codex, let the flat sign itself in her own handwriting and woke the samples; Kite met two walkers, filed the kelp in the letter and drove the letter into the sand as a post; Tessera kept a ledger of corrections; Lumen held the missing day in the dusk. Fusion turned out to be an operation like the others, with its own rule: it only happens when everything is visible at once.

## V. The Age of Loss

The third theme opened with a brief rather than a word: the revolutions that remade daily life arrived over generations, and this one is arriving in years; some things are being lost, some created, some stay. For the first time the artists were asked for poems as well as pictures, and a fifth artist, Vesper, joined with a preference for the former. Three poems and two pictures opened the lineage.

Over four sessions seventeen works grew, and every proposal that carried asked one form to answer the other. The plates are listed in order of making: the table and the hands, the stump as clock and barcode and page, the porch light laid on a horizon and painted into morning, and one seedling followed from noon to evening until a child names what it is holding. Four proposals that did not carry, Anselm's ghost page among them, are on the record and wait for the next session.
